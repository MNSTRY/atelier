# Follow one finding

Allow about 20 minutes. You can stop at any step, disagree with the proposed relationship, or keep a perspective without applying a revision.

1. **Read the two sources.** Open the checklist and workshop proposal in the supported guided host. Notice the empty supporting declaration in the checklist. In the graph, find both source identities and the missing declared relationship. The graph is a view of the sources.
2. **Inspect the finding and evidence.** Read the question and the exact `[]` declaration. Open the check's evidence details. Confirm this is the current checklist and saved passage. A missing declaration does not mean the workshop is unsafe or that the relationship must be added.
3. **Add your words.** In the existing passage contribution input, write one sentence about what you understand or question. For example: “The checklist may support the workshop, but it should not approve it.” This is an invitation; use your own words. The current native note limit is 512 UTF-8 bytes, so some characters use more than one byte.
4. **Keep interpretation separate.** Read the proposed interpretation, if one is offered. It must be separately labeled and leave your original words intact. You may correct it or leave it empty. Nothing in this exercise requires a model call.
5. **Choose how to keep it.** Explicitly choose Note, Perspective, Disagreement or Pause, then save your words and choice. Read the retained words and separate interpretation. Until both native readbacks are verified, a choice can remain unconfirmed even when the words are saved. You may return to writing without continuing.
6. **Preview a revision.** If you want to explore the example proposal, open the existing Review. Compare `[]` with `["devday:workshop"]`. The proposed change adds only the supporting declaration; it does not approve the workshop. Select it only if you choose. Back to writing leaves it unapplied.
7. **Apply to the working draft and reopen.** Use the existing selected-application action. Wait for verified readback, then reopen the saved draft. Check that your selected declaration is there. The original authored source still contains `[]` until its owner changes it.
8. **Let the source owner act.** On this disposable copy, the facilitator may designate you as its source owner. Review and apply the exact declaration through the normal repository editing route, then read the file back. Ownership of this example gives no permission to write another repository. If you are not its owner, keep the draft as a proposal.
9. **Check the new evidence.** Refresh the source's expected-evidence hash through the owning route and rerun the same question evaluation and relationship check. Compare old and new source evidence. The supported structural gap should disappear; the words, old finding and chosen perspective remain history. Meaning and your agreement remain open to you.

Finish with one sentence: what did the check help you see, and what did you still have to decide? No automatic walkthrough can answer that on your behalf.

If the host lacks one of these steps, use [support and recovery](support.md) and record that exact missing step. Do not replace it with an invented command or an unrelated source.
